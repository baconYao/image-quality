## Resolution comparison: 1920x1080 vs 3840x2160 (same synthetic testsrc content)

Both datasets share the same procedurally-generated pattern (color bars, gradient, shapes/text), scaled to 1080p and 4K respectively, and are put through the identical noise/blur PSNR-calibrated sweep and transcode-corruption set. Since noise/blur are calibrated to hit the *same target PSNR* at both resolutions, this isolates how resolution alone shifts the perceptual (SSIM/VMAF) reading of an equal-PSNR distortion.

**Observation**: at matched target PSNR, VMAF direction depends on the distortion type. For noise, VMAF at 4K is close to (slightly below) 1080p. For blur, VMAF drops noticeably more at 4K than at 1080p (e.g. target PSNR 30 dB: VMAF 56.19 at 1080p vs 43.79 at 4K) -- the same Gaussian blur sigma calibrated to a fixed PSNR removes proportionally more fine detail relative to a 4K frame's higher native sharpness expectation, so VMAF (which models perceived sharpness loss) penalizes it harder. For the golden-derived transcode corruptions, VMAF is consistently a few points *higher* at 4K for the same corruption (block_glitch, heavy_compression, chroma_swap), since a fixed-size corrupted region/JPEG block covers a smaller relative fraction of a larger 4K frame. PSNR/SSIM stay close between resolutions in both cases since those metrics operate on relative/local pixel neighborhoods rather than absolute pixel counts, unlike VMAF's resolution-aware perceptual model.

## Cross-validation: ffmpeg CLI vs OpenCV

To make sure the pure-OpenCV metric implementations used everywhere in this report are trustworthy, `scripts/compare_ffmpeg_vs_opencv.py` independently recomputes PSNR/SSIM with ffmpeg's native `psnr`/`ssim` CLI filters (system `ffmpeg`, no libvmaf support on this machine) and compares MS-SSIM/VMAF against the standalone libvmaf binary. Full per-sample tables: [`output/ffmpeg_vs_opencv.md`](output/ffmpeg_vs_opencv.md), [`output_bbb/ffmpeg_vs_opencv.md`](output_bbb/ffmpeg_vs_opencv.md).

| Dataset | Samples | PSNR WARN (>0.5dB delta) | SSIM WARN (>0.05 delta) | MS-SSIM WARN (>0.05 delta) | Max ΔPSNR | Max ΔSSIM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| testsrc | 16 | 0/16 | 0/16 | 11/16 | 0.000 dB | 0.0481 |
| Big Buck Bunny | 16 | 0/16 | 3/16 | 7/16 | 0.000 dB | 0.0862 |

**Conclusion**: PSNR is numerically identical between OpenCV (`cv2.PSNR`) and ffmpeg (`-lavfi psnr`) on every sample tested (same MSE-based formula). SSIM agrees within ~0.01-0.05 (different default window: OpenCV's 11x11 Gaussian vs ffmpeg's 8x8 uniform -- both are valid SSIM variants). MS-SSIM shows the largest divergence from libvmaf's `float_ms_ssim`, especially on solid-color anchors and chroma-only corruption -- treat MS-SSIM as directionally useful but not numerically interchangeable across implementations. VMAF has no independent second implementation to diff against here.

## Key findings

1. **Same PSNR != same perceived quality.** At PSNR ~20 dB, noise still scores VMAF ~65-69 while blur is floored at VMAF 0 on both datasets -- PSNR alone cannot distinguish these two very differently-perceived distortions.

2. **Blur is far more damaging per-dB than noise.** Blur rarely or never reaches VMAF>=80 even at PSNR 30 dB, while noise stays acceptable down to roughly PSNR 24-26 dB.

3. **Color-only corruption (chroma_loss / chroma_swap) breaks the opposite way**: PSNR is very low (7-17 dB, looks catastrophic to a PSNR-only gate) yet VMAF is very high (92-97), because VMAF's model weighs luma far more than chroma. A human viewer would immediately notice a grayscale or color-cast frame, but VMAF would rate it as near-perfect.

4. **Structural corruption (row tearing, block glitch, packet loss) is scored more consistently across metrics** -- both PSNR and VMAF agree these are visibly bad, though the exact severity ranking differs (e.g. row_tearing scores worst on VMAF despite not having the lowest PSNR of the broken set).

5. **Practical implication**: no single metric is safe to use alone as an automated quality gate across arbitrary distortion types. A combination of PSNR (cheap, consistent trend) + SSIM/MS-SSIM (structural) + VMAF (perceptual, luma-heavy) gives a much more complete picture, and color-integrity should probably be checked separately (e.g. mean chroma delta) since none of these three metrics reliably flags pure color-cast/desaturation issues.
